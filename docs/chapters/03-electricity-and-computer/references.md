# References: Electricity and the Single-Board Computer

1. [Raspberry Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raspberry_Pi) - Wikipedia - Tells the history of the Raspberry Pi, from a tool for teaching children programming to a worldwide single-board computer. Its model tables compare boards, including the Zero 2 W used in this station.

2. [General-purpose input/output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-purpose_input/output) - Wikipedia - Explains what GPIO pins are, how software sets them as inputs or outputs, and their voltage limits. It gives background for the chapter's section on pins, headers, and safe wiring.

3. [Electrostatic discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic_discharge) - Wikipedia - Describes how static charge builds up and suddenly jumps between objects, and how it damages electronics. Its section on prevention explains why grounding yourself before touching a board matters.

4. Conceptual Physics - Paul G. Hewitt - Pearson - Hewitt pioneered teaching physics through ideas and pictures before equations. His treatment of electricity describes voltage as "electrical pressure" with simple water analogies, giving students a mental model for voltage, current, and ground.

5. Make: Electronics - Charles Platt - Maker Media - Platt invented a "learning by discovery" approach in which readers first build and even break circuits, such as burning out an LED on purpose, then learn the theory. It suits hands-on breadboard work.

6. [Raspberry Pi Computer Hardware](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/raspberry-pi.html) - Raspberry Pi Documentation - The official hardware guide, including the "GPIO and the 40-pin header" section. It shows the pin layout, the 3.3-volt logic level, and power pins students must understand before wiring a sensor.

7. [Raspberry Pi GPIO Pinout](https://pinout.xyz/) - Pinout.xyz - An interactive map of the 40-pin header where clicking any pin shows its number, name, and functions. Students can quickly find power, ground, and I2C pins while wiring their station.

8. [Breadboards for Beginners](https://learn.adafruit.com/breadboards-for-beginners) - Adafruit Learning System - A photo-rich guide showing how breadboard rows and power rails are connected inside. Close-up pictures and simple example circuits help students make their first solderless connections with jumper wires correctly.

9. [Pull-up Resistors](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pull-up-resistors/all) - SparkFun Learn - Explains why an unconnected input "floats" and how a pull-up resistor holds it at a known voltage. Simple diagrams and a worked example connect directly to the chapter's pull-up resistor section.

10. [Circuit Construction Kit: DC](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/circuit-construction-kit-dc) - PhET Interactive Simulations - A free simulation where students build circuits with batteries, wires, bulbs, and resistors and watch current flow. Virtual meters let them measure voltage and current safely before touching real hardware.
