# References: Ground Motion: Measuring Earthquakes

1. [Seismic wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_wave) - Wikipedia - Explains P waves, S waves, and surface waves, including how fast each travels and how they move the ground. Its animations and diagrams support the chapter's comparison of the fast P wave and damaging S wave.

2. [Seismometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismometer) - Wikipedia - Traces seismic instruments from Zhang Heng's seismoscope to John Milne's seismograph and modern broadband sensors. It explains the inertial-mass principle that connects every instrument in this chapter, including MEMS accelerometers.

3. [Seismic magnitude scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_magnitude_scales) - Wikipedia - Compares the Richter scale, moment magnitude, and other scales, and explains how they differ from intensity scales. It is a strong reference for the chapter's key distinction between magnitude and intensity.

4. Earthquakes - Bruce A. Bolt - W. H. Freeman - Bolt, longtime director of Berkeley's seismographic stations, is widely praised for making seismology unusually clear. He walks readers through reading a seismogram and finding an earthquake's distance and magnitude with simple worked examples and diagrams.

5. Richter's Scale: Measure of an Earthquake, Measure of a Man - Susan Elizabeth Hough - Princeton University Press - USGS seismologist Hough explains how Charles Richter and Beno Gutenberg built the first magnitude scale in 1935. Her account shows why a single number for earthquake size was such a powerful idea.

6. [Earthquake Magnitude, Energy Release, and Shaking Intensity](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/earthquake-magnitude-energy-release-and-shaking-intensity) - U.S. Geological Survey - Explains why each earthquake has one magnitude but many intensity values, and how energy grows about 32 times per magnitude step. It directly supports the chapter's magnitude versus intensity section.

7. [Seismic Wave Motions: 4 Waves Animated](https://www.iris.edu/hq/inclass/animation/seismic_wave_motions4_waves_animated) - IRIS / EarthScope - A short classroom animation showing how P, S, Love, and Rayleigh waves move the ground differently. Teachers can play it to help students picture motions that are hard to imagine from text alone.

8. [Seismology](https://www.mtu.edu/geo/community/seismology/learn/seismology-study/) - Michigan Technological University - A university outreach page explaining seismic waves, how seismographs work, and how magnitude is measured. Linked pages show how to make your own P and S waves and how to locate an epicenter.

9. [ShakeAlert](https://www.shakealert.org/) - ShakeAlert - The official earthquake early warning system for California, Oregon, and Washington, run by the USGS and partners. It explains how sensors detect the first P waves and send alerts seconds before strong shaking arrives.

10. [MyShake](https://myshake.berkeley.edu/) - UC Berkeley Seismology Lab - Describes the free MyShake app, which delivers earthquake warnings and uses smartphone accelerometers to collect shaking data. It shows how ordinary MEMS sensors can join a real early warning network.
