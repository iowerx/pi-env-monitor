# References: Charting and Interpreting Your Data

1. [Moving average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_average) - Wikipedia - Explains simple and weighted moving averages and how they smooth noisy data to reveal trends. Graphs comparing raw and smoothed data show exactly what happens when students average their station's readings.

2. [Correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation) - Wikipedia - Describes how correlation measures whether two quantities rise and fall together, including the correlation coefficient. Its famous scatter plot examples and warnings that correlation is not causation support the chapter's analysis section.

3. [Outlier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlier) - Wikipedia - Explains what outliers are, what causes them, and common ways to detect them. It helps students decide whether a strange reading is a sensor fault or a real event, a key data quality skill.

4. The Visual Display of Quantitative Information - Edward R. Tufte - Graphics Press - Tufte introduced the "data-ink ratio" and the term "chartjunk," arguing that every mark on a graph should show data. His principles give students a clear test for making honest, uncluttered line charts.

5. How to Lie with Statistics - Darrell Huff - W. W. Norton - Huff's famous "gee-whiz graph" shows how cutting off the bottom of an axis makes a small change look dramatic. His short, funny examples teach students to label axes honestly and read charts skeptically.

6. [Pyplot Tutorial](https://matplotlib.org/stable/tutorials/pyplot.html) - Matplotlib - The official introduction to making line charts and scatter plots in Python with Matplotlib. Step-by-step examples show how to add axis labels, titles, and multiple data series to a chart.

7. [Pressure](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/winds/pressure_winds/Pressure.htm) - National Weather Service Houston/Galveston - A training page explaining what makes barometric pressure rise and fall and what each change means for the weather. It supports the chapter's pressure tendency and barometric forecasting section.

8. [Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/aboutsshws.php) - NOAA National Hurricane Center - Describes the five hurricane categories, based on sustained wind speed, with the damage expected at each level. It shows why the difference between sustained winds and gusts matters for warnings.

9. [Detection of Outliers](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm) - NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods - Explains common methods for finding outliers, including box plots and statistical tests. It gives teachers and advanced students a reliable reference for the chapter's data validation work.

10. [Spurious Correlations](https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations) - Tyler Vigen - A collection of charts showing unrelated things that happen to rise and fall together, such as odd pairs of statistics. The humorous examples make it memorable that correlation alone never proves cause.
