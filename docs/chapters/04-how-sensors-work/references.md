# References: How Sensors Turn the World Into Numbers

1. [Transducer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transducer) - Wikipedia - Defines a transducer as a device that changes one form of energy into another, with many sensor examples. It supports the chapter's central idea that every sensor works by transduction.

2. [Analog-to-digital converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog-to-digital_converter) - Wikipedia - Explains how an ADC samples a continuous voltage and turns it into whole numbers, including resolution and sampling rate. Its diagrams of quantization show where a sensor's smallest step comes from.

3. [MEMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEMS) - Wikipedia - Describes microelectromechanical systems, tiny moving structures carved into silicon chips. Its examples of pressure sensors and accelerometers connect to the sensing mechanisms inside this station's own chips.

4. Code: The Hidden Language of Computer Hardware and Software - Charles Petzold - Microsoft Press - Petzold's famous approach starts with two friends signaling with flashlights and builds, step by step, to a working computer. It makes the jump from on-and-off signals to digital numbers easy to follow.

5. Make: Sensors - Tero Karvinen, Kimmo Karvinen, and Ville Valtokari - Maker Media - The authors pair each sensor with a short, working project, wiring diagram, and code for both Arduino and Raspberry Pi. This build-it-first approach shows students how a datasheet becomes a working reading.

6. [Sensors and Transducers](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/io/io_1.html) - Electronics Tutorials - An introduction to sensors and transducers, active and passive sensors, and analog versus digital outputs. Its clear diagrams and signal conditioning section explain why small sensor signals are amplified and filtered.

7. [Analog to Digital Conversion](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/analog-to-digital-conversion/all) - SparkFun Learn - A beginner tutorial explaining how a microcontroller measures a voltage and reports it as a number. Worked examples show how reference voltage and bit depth set the resolution of each reading.

8. [Physical Computing with Python](https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/physical-computing) - Raspberry Pi Foundation - A free step-by-step project that wires LEDs, buttons, and sensors to a Raspberry Pi. Its light-dependent resistor and analogue input steps show a real sensor signal becoming a number in Python.

9. [The Electromagnetic Spectrum](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/) - NASA Science - A NASA tour of radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. Each wave type has its own illustrated page, giving background for light and infrared sensors.

10. [Photoelectric Effect](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/photoelectric) - PhET Interactive Simulations - An interactive simulation where students shine light of different colors on a metal and watch electrons escape. Changing brightness and wavelength shows why light sensors respond to some light and not other light.
