# References: Solar Radiation: The Energy That Drives the Weather

1. [Solar irradiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_irradiance) - Wikipedia - Explains irradiance in watts per square meter, the solar constant, and how sunlight changes with angle and atmosphere. Its maps and graphs of daily and yearly sunlight support the chapter's measurement ideas.

2. [Ultraviolet index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_index) - Wikipedia - Describes how the UV index is calculated from sunlight and what each number means for sunburn risk. Its color-coded scale and history help students read and explain a UV forecast.

3. [Albedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo) - Wikipedia - Explains albedo, the fraction of sunlight a surface reflects, with values for snow, forest, ocean, and pavement. Its maps and examples show why different surfaces heat up so differently in the Sun.

4. QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter - Richard P. Feynman - Princeton University Press - Feynman explains light as particles called photons using only drawings of "little arrows," with no advanced math. This famous approach helps readers understand why light can knock electrons loose in a solar cell.

5. Let It Shine: The 6,000-Year Story of Solar Energy - John Perlin - New World Library - Perlin traces solar energy from ancient sun-facing buildings to the 1954 Bell Labs silicon solar cell. His storytelling links the photovoltaic effect to the real people and experiments that made solar power practical.

6. [Climate and Earth's Energy Budget](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-and-earths-energy-budget/) - NASA Earth Observatory - A multi-page NASA feature explaining how much sunlight reaches Earth, how much is reflected, and how much is absorbed. Clear diagrams of incoming and outgoing energy connect irradiance, albedo, and climate.

7. [UV Index Scale](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/uv-index-scale-0) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Explains each level of the UV index scale, from low to extreme, with recommended sun protection. Students can compare their station's UV readings to these health guidelines.

8. [Solar Calculator](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/) - NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory - An interactive tool that calculates sunrise, sunset, and the Sun's elevation and zenith angle for any place and date. Students can predict daily and seasonal sunlight patterns at their own station.

9. [Herschel's Experiment](https://coolcosmos.ipac.caltech.edu/page/herschel_experiment) - Caltech IPAC Cool Cosmos - Tells how William Herschel used a prism and blackened thermometers in 1800 to discover infrared light. It notes the experiment is simple enough to repeat on a sunny day with common materials, linking history to hands-on science.

10. [How Does Solar Work?](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/how-does-solar-work) - U.S. Department of Energy - An introduction to photovoltaic and solar-thermal technologies, with links to deeper basics pages. It helps students connect the photovoltaic effect in this chapter to real solar panels and power systems.
