# References: Wind: Measuring Air in Motion

1. [Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) - Wikipedia - Explains wind as air moving from high to low pressure, shaped by the pressure gradient, Earth's rotation, and friction. It covers measurement, scales, and wind's effects on people, matching nearly every section of this chapter.

2. [Anemometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemometer) - Wikipedia - Describes the main types of wind-speed instruments, including cup, vane, hot-wire, and sonic anemometers. Photographs and explanations of how each works support the chapter's anemometer families section.

3. [Beaufort scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_scale) - Wikipedia - Presents the full Beaufort scale from calm to hurricane force, with wind speeds in knots and meters per second. Its descriptions of sea and land conditions show how wind can be estimated without instruments.

4. Defining the Wind - Scott Huler - Crown - Huler traces how Admiral Francis Beaufort's scale grew from sailors' notes into a precise, almost poetic standard of observation. His storytelling shows students how careful description can become a measurement scale anyone can use.

5. Storm Kings - Lee Sandlin - Pantheon Books - Sandlin tells the story of America's first storm scientists, including the bitter debate between James Espy and William Redfield over whether storms spin. It brings the science of pressure systems and wind to life through vivid history.

6. [The Highs and Lows of Air Pressure](https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/how-weather-works/highs-and-lows-air-pressure) - UCAR Center for Science Education - Explains high and low pressure systems, why winds swirl around them, and how they appear on weather maps. Clear diagrams help students connect pressure readings to the weather they bring.

7. [Origin of Wind](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/synoptic/origin-of-wind) - NOAA JetStream - Explains how the pressure gradient, the Coriolis effect, and friction work together to steer wind. Weather map examples show how tightly spaced isobars mean stronger winds, a skill students can practice.

8. [Beaufort Wind Force Scale](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/guides/coast-and-sea/beaufort-scale) - Met Office - The UK weather service's marine Beaufort table, listing each force with wind speed in knots and meters per second, wave heights, and sea descriptions. Its conversion note helps students switch between knots and meters per second.

9. [Understanding Wind Chill](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart) - National Weather Service - Explains how wind makes cold air feel colder and includes the official wind chill chart and a calculator. Its frostbite guidance connects the chapter's apparent temperature section to real safety decisions.

10. [Enhanced Fujita Scale](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/efscale/) - NOAA Storm Prediction Center - Describes how tornadoes are rated from EF0 to EF5 by studying damage to buildings and trees. Its damage indicators show how wind speed is estimated after the fact from wind load on structures.
