# References: The Language of Measurement

1. [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) - Wikipedia - Covers the seven SI base units, derived units, and prefixes, plus their history. Its tables of units and prefixes are a handy reference when converting any reading the station records.

2. [Accuracy and precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accuracy_and_precision) - Wikipedia - Explains the difference between being close to the true value and being repeatable. Its target diagrams make the chapter's main idea, that accuracy and precision are not the same, easy to see.

3. [Measurement uncertainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_uncertainty) - Wikipedia - Describes how scientists state the doubt attached to every measured number. It connects uncertainty to calibration and reference standards, supporting the chapter's vocabulary of measurement quality.

4. An Introduction to Error Analysis - John R. Taylor - University Science Books - Taylor's famous opening example, testing whether a crown is real gold, shows why a measurement means nothing without its uncertainty. His gentle worked approach made error analysis teachable to beginning science students.

5. Powers of Ten - Philip Morrison and Phylis Morrison - Scientific American Library - Based on the Eames film, the Morrisons zoom from galaxies to atoms, one factor of ten per page. This picture-by-picture approach made orders of magnitude and scientific notation something readers can actually see.

6. [SI Units](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/si-units) - NIST - The U.S. measurement agency's guide to the seven SI base units and how each is defined. Links lead to pages for length, mass, temperature, and time, with clear examples suited to classroom use.

7. [SI Prefixes](https://www.bipm.org/en/measurement-units/si-prefixes) - BIPM - The international bureau that maintains the SI lists every official prefix, from quecto to quetta, with symbols and powers of ten. Students can use it to practice unit conversion and connect prefixes to scientific notation.

8. [Accuracy and Precision](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_%28CK-12%29/03%3A_Measurements/3.12%3A_Accuracy_and_Precision) - LibreTexts - A free open-textbook lesson that uses dartboard pictures to separate accuracy from precision. Its examples of repeated lab measurements help students judge their own sensor readings using this chapter's vocabulary.

9. [Cell Size and Scale](https://learn.genetics.utah.edu/content/cells/scale/) - University of Utah Genetic Science Learning Center - An interactive slider that zooms from objects you can see with your eyes down to a single carbon atom. It gives students a hands-on feel for orders of magnitude and why scientists need scientific notation.

10. [Scientific Notation](https://www.mathsisfun.com/numbers/scientific-notation.html) - Math Is Fun - A friendly, step-by-step lesson on writing very large and very small numbers as powers of ten. Worked examples and short practice questions help students who need extra support with this chapter's math.
