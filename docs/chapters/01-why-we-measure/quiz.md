# Quiz: Why We Measure the Natural Environment

Test your understanding of environmental monitoring, observing versus measuring, the atmosphere, and the difference between weather and climate with these review questions.

!!! mascot-tip "Before You Start"
    ![Mecha pointing out a tip](../../img/mascot/tip.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Read each question carefully and pick your answer before you open the explanation. Check the data, then check it again!

---

#### 1. Which of the following statements is an example of quantitative data?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. "The sky is overcast this morning."
2. "Relative humidity is 88 percent."
3. "The temperature is 25."
4. "It is colder today than it was yesterday."
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Quantitative data needs both a number and a unit, and "88 percent" has both. Options A and D describe the weather in words with no number, so they are qualitative observations. Option C is the tricky one. It has a number, but no unit. Is it 25 degrees Celsius or Fahrenheit? Without a unit, nobody can compare it to anything.

    **Concept Tested:** Quantitative Data

    **See:** [Observing Versus Measuring](index.md#observing-versus-measuring)

#### 2. Which statement best describes environmental monitoring as this chapter defines it?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Taking one careful reading of the outdoor air with a calibrated instrument
2. Describing outdoor conditions in a field notebook using plain words
3. Running a short laboratory experiment to answer one question about air
4. Measuring outdoor conditions repeatedly, in the same place, over a long time
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Environmental monitoring means measuring conditions in the natural environment over and over, in the same place, for a long time. A single reading (A) is only a snapshot. Plain-word notes (B) describe instead of measuring. A short lab experiment (C) happens indoors and ends in an afternoon. Monitoring answers questions that take months or years of readings.

    **Concept Tested:** Environmental Monitoring

    **See:** [What Environmental Monitoring Is](index.md#what-environmental-monitoring-is)

#### 3. In which atmospheric layer does all weather happen?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Troposphere
2. Stratosphere
3. Mesosphere
4. Thermosphere
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The troposphere is the lowest atmospheric layer. It reaches from the ground up to about 12 km, and all weather happens there. Your station sits in the first few meters of it. The stratosphere (B) holds the ozone layer, the mesosphere (C) is where most meteors burn up, and the thermosphere (D) is where auroras glow and the space station orbits.

    **Concept Tested:** Atmospheric Layer

    **See:** [The Air Above You](index.md#the-air-above-you)

---

#### 4. Which gas makes up about 78 percent of Earth's atmosphere?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Oxygen
2. Carbon dioxide
3. Nitrogen
4. Argon
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The atmosphere is the layer of gases held around Earth by gravity, and it is about 78 percent nitrogen. Oxygen (A) is the next largest part, at about 21 percent. Carbon dioxide (B) and argon (D) belong to the last one percent, along with water vapor. That small one percent does a surprising amount of the interesting work in the atmosphere.

    **Concept Tested:** Atmosphere

    **See:** [The Air Above You](index.md#the-air-above-you)

#### 5. Why is "scary" not a physical property?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Different people would describe it using different words
2. It changes too quickly for any instrument to keep up with it
3. There is nothing physical to compare against an agreed standard
4. It can only be studied indoors, not in the natural environment
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A physical property is a feature of an object or place that can be described by a number. Measuring means comparing something to an agreed standard, like comparing a table to a meter stick. "Scary" has nothing physical to compare, so nobody can build an instrument for it. Option A is true, but it is a result of the problem, not the cause. Options B and D are not the reason.

    **Concept Tested:** Physical Property

    **See:** [Observing Versus Measuring](index.md#observing-versus-measuring)

#### 6. In the three-bowl experiment, the middle bowl feels hot to one hand and cold to the other. What does this show about human senses?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Senses report change from before, not an actual amount
2. The left hand is more sensitive to heat than the right hand
3. Skin measures temperature accurately but more slowly than a thermometer
4. Warm water carries heat into the skin faster than cold water does
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Each hand compares the middle bowl to the bowl it just left. One hand says "warmer than before," and the other says "colder than before." Both are telling the truth, but neither one is measuring. A thermometer compares the water to an agreed standard and reports a number, such as 21 °C. That is the same for everyone. Options B, C, and D miss the point. Skin reports change, not amount.

    **Concept Tested:** Measurement

    **See:** [It Is Cold Outside](index.md#it-is-cold-outside)

#### 7. According to the chapter, what usually causes the readings at a monitoring station to change from day to day?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The troposphere grows thicker and thinner over the course of each day
2. The Sun's energy output rises and falls sharply from one day to the next
3. A single body of air stays in place and slowly warms up and cools down
4. Different air masses, each with its own temperature and moisture, arrive and leave
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. An air mass is a huge body of air with roughly the same temperature and moisture throughout. It gets its character from where it formed. Air over the Gulf of Mexico becomes warm and wet, and air over northern Canada becomes cold and dry. Your station is usually watching different air masses arrive and depart, not one body of air warming and cooling in place (C). Options A and B are not what drives day-to-day change.

    **Concept Tested:** Air Mass

    **See:** [The Air Above You](index.md#the-air-above-you)

#### 8. Which task best describes the work of a meteorologist rather than a climatologist?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Studying tree rings to learn how rainfall changed over 300 years
2. Forecasting whether a storm will close the airport tomorrow
3. Analyzing 50 years of records to see if a valley is getting drier
4. Reading gas bubbles in ice cores to reconstruct past temperatures
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A meteorologist studies and forecasts weather over short time scales, from hours to about two weeks. Deciding whether a storm will close an airport is a forecast, so it is meteorology. Options A, C, and D all look at patterns over decades or centuries, using long records, tree rings, and ice cores. That is the work of a climatologist. The two fields use much of the same data, but they ask different questions.

    **Concept Tested:** Meteorologist and Climatologist

    **See:** [The People Who Do This Work](index.md#the-people-who-do-this-work)

#### 9. Your station records the hottest June day in its three-year history. A classmate says this proves the local climate is warming. What is the best response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. One hot day is weather; a claim about climate needs decades of readings
2. The classmate is right, because record highs can only happen in a warming climate
3. The classmate is right as long as the sensor was properly calibrated that day
4. The claim can be checked by comparing that day with the hottest day last June
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Weather is the state of the atmosphere at one place and time. Climate is the pattern of weather averaged over a long time, usually 30 years. One record day, even an accurate one, is weather. Extreme days happen inside normal variation (B is wrong). Calibration (C) makes the reading trustworthy, but it does not turn one day into a trend. Comparing two days (D) is still just weather.

    **Concept Tested:** Weather and Climate

    **See:** [Weather Is Not Climate](index.md#weather-is-not-climate)

#### 10. Two students keep weather notes all March. One writes "windy" on windy days. The other records the wind speed in km/h. A year later, only the second student can tell whether this March was windier than last. What best explains why?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The second student's notes are longer, so they hold more information
2. The word "windy" is not real information about what the wind was doing
3. Numbers with units can be compared and graphed; plain-word notes cannot
4. The second student used an instrument, and instruments never make mistakes
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A qualitative observation like "windy" is real, useful information, so B is wrong. Qualitative notes are how you notice that something needs measuring. But "windy" cannot be graphed or compared with last year, while 24 km/h can. Length of notes (A) does not matter. Instruments can drift and fail (D), which is why later chapters teach you to check them.

    **Concept Tested:** Qualitative Observation

    **See:** [Observing Versus Measuring](index.md#observing-versus-measuring)
