# References: Time and Place: Recording Where and When

1. [History of longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude) - Wikipedia - Traces the centuries-long struggle to find longitude at sea, from lunar distances to Harrison's marine chronometer. It expands the chapter's story of the longitude problem with maps, portraits, and clear explanations.

2. [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) - Wikipedia - A detailed article on how GPS satellites, atomic clocks, and receivers work together to find a position. Its sections on trilateration, accuracy, and the WGS 84 datum match the chapter closely.

3. [Coordinated Universal Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time) - Wikipedia - Explains UTC, the world's shared time standard, and how it relates to atomic time, leap seconds, and time zones. It shows why remote stations record UTC instead of local time.

4. Longitude - Dava Sobel - Walker - Sobel turned a technical navigation problem into a gripping human story of clockmaker John Harrison against the astronomers. Her storytelling approach made the link between accurate clocks and finding position memorable for general readers.

5. From Sundials to Atomic Clocks - James Jespersen and Jane Fitz-Randolph - Dover Publications - Written by a National Bureau of Standards time scientist and a science writer, this book explains timekeeping with simple drawings and everyday examples. It is widely praised for making atomic clocks and time standards unusually clear.

6. [Longitude Found: The Story of Harrison's Timekeepers](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/time/harrisons-clocks-longitude-problem) - Royal Museums Greenwich - The museum that holds Harrison's clocks tells how H1 through H4 solved the longitude problem. Photographs of the real timekeepers help students see the marine chronometer this chapter describes.

7. [What Is Longitude?](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/longitude.html) - NOAA Ocean Service - A short explainer on longitude, meridians, and the 1884 choice of Greenwich as the prime meridian. It also explains why a GPS reads zero longitude about 102 meters east of the Greenwich markers.

8. [How Does GPS Work?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/gps/en/) - NASA Space Place - A kid-friendly NASA page explaining how a receiver uses signals from several satellites to find its location. Simple illustrations and a game make trilateration easy for middle school students to grasp.

9. [GPS Accuracy](https://www.gps.gov/gps-accuracy) - GPS.gov - The official U.S. government explanation of how accurate GPS is and why. It lists real accuracy numbers and explains how buildings, trees, and the atmosphere affect a position fix.

10. [How Is UTC(NIST) Related to Coordinated Universal Time?](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/how-utcnist-related-coordinated-universal-time-utc-international) - NIST - The national time lab explains how UTC, atomic time, Greenwich Mean Time, GPS time, and Zulu time relate. It helps students choose and label timestamps correctly in their station's data.
