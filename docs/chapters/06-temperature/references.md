# References: Temperature: From the Thermoscope to the Silicon Chip

1. [Temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature) - Wikipedia - Explains temperature as a measure of the average motion energy of particles, along with scales and absolute zero. It supports the chapter's opening idea that temperature is atomic motion, not a substance.

2. [Thermometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermometer) - Wikipedia - Traces thermometers from Galileo's thermoscope through liquid-in-glass, resistance, and electronic designs. Its history and photographs follow the same instrument lineage the chapter describes, ending at modern electronic sensors.

3. [Thermistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermistor) - Wikipedia - Describes thermistors, resistors whose resistance changes strongly with temperature, and how they are used as sensors. Its graphs and equations show how a resistance reading becomes a temperature in software.

4. The Feynman Lectures on Physics - Richard P. Feynman - Basic Books - Feynman's opening lecture, "Atoms in Motion," pictures heat as the jiggling of atoms, using magnified drops of water. This vivid image gives students a clear model of temperature as kinetic energy of atoms.

5. Inventing Temperature - Hasok Chang - Oxford University Press - Chang reconstructs how scientists set fixed points, like boiling water, before any trusted thermometer existed. His historical detective work shows why defining temperature scales was a real scientific achievement, not an obvious step.

6. [Kelvin: Introduction](https://www.nist.gov/si-redefinition/kelvin-introduction) - NIST - Explains the Kelvin scale, absolute zero, and how the kelvin was redefined using the Boltzmann constant. Clear comparisons with Celsius and Fahrenheit, including room temperature in all three, support the chapter's conversions.

7. [States of Matter: Basics](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/states-of-matter-basics) - PhET Interactive Simulations - A free simulation where students heat and cool atoms and watch them speed up, slow down, and change state. A built-in thermometer links particle motion directly to temperature readings.

8. [Thermistor](https://learn.adafruit.com/thermistor) - Adafruit Learning System - A hands-on guide to wiring a thermistor, reading it with a microcontroller, and converting resistance to temperature. Example code and wiring diagrams show the electronic thermometer in action.

9. [Make Your Own Thermometer Box](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/met-office-for-schools/other-content/other-resources/weather-station/thermometer) - Met Office - A classroom activity for building a simple white, slatted shelter for a thermometer. It explains why real Stevenson screens are white, ventilated, and north-facing, linking directly to sensor exposure.

10. [Temperature Unit Converter](https://www.weather.gov/epz/wxcalc_tempconvert) - National Weather Service - An online calculator that converts between Fahrenheit, Celsius, Kelvin, and Rankine. Students can use it to check their own hand conversions between the three scales covered in this chapter.
