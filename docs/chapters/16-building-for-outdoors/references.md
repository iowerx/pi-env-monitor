# References: Building the Station for the Outdoors

1. [Weather station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_station) - Wikipedia - Describes the instruments in land and ocean weather stations and the rules for placing them. Its notes on siting, shelters, and automatic stations connect directly to the chapter's sensor siting and exposure section.

2. [IP code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_code) - Wikipedia - Explains the ingress protection rating system, where two digits describe protection against dust and water. Its tables let students decode a rating like IP65 when choosing a weatherproof enclosure.

3. [Telemetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemetry) - Wikipedia - Describes how measurements are collected at remote places and sent automatically to a receiving station. Examples from weather, space, and environmental monitoring show where this station's cellular link fits.

4. The Weather Observer's Handbook - Stephen Burt - Cambridge University Press - Burt is widely praised for practical, tested guidance on siting and exposing instruments, including compromise sites at homes and schools. His comparisons show how placement errors change readings, matching this chapter's siting advice.

5. Solar Electricity Handbook - Michael Boxwell - Greenstream Publishing - Boxwell teaches solar system design through a step-by-step method that starts with a daily power budget and works out panel, battery, and controller sizes. Its worked examples suit students planning a station's power.

6. [Weather Stations](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/how-forecasts-are-made/observations/weather-stations) - Met Office - Explains how the UK's automatic weather stations are sited and run, including ideal and undesirable locations. Its examples of trees, buildings, and frost hollows show how a poor site distorts readings.

7. [Build Your Own Weather Station](https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/build-your-own-weather-station) - Raspberry Pi Foundation - A free, step-by-step project for building a Raspberry Pi weather station with temperature, humidity, wind, and rain sensors. Its steps on assembling for outdoors and keeping electronics dry match this chapter.

8. [Li-Ion and LiPoly Batteries](https://learn.adafruit.com/li-ion-and-lipoly-batteries) - Adafruit Learning System - Explains the two main kinds of rechargeable lithium batteries, their voltages, and how to charge and protect them safely. Example packs rated in milliamp-hours help students compare battery capacity for their power budget.

9. [MQTT: The Standard for IoT Messaging](https://mqtt.org/) - MQTT.org - Introduces MQTT, a lightweight messaging system designed for remote devices on slow or unreliable networks. It shows how a station can send readings to a base station even when its connection comes and goes.

10. [ANSS: Advanced National Seismic System](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/anss-advanced-national-seismic-system) - U.S. Geological Survey - Describes the national system of more than 3,000 seismic stations and 15 regional networks. It shows how many stations working together locate earthquakes and power early warning, as the chapter explains.
